00 // EXPERIMENTAL FOUNDATION

Research Methodology & Scientific Protocols

Structured electrophysiological pipelines, controlled recording environments, and leakage-resistant validation frameworks governing BrainWave's investigation into imagined-speech neural dynamics.

01 EXPERIMENTAL CONTROLS

Controlled Speech Imagery

Defines reproducible trial structures for investigating scalp EEG correlates of imagined speech. Protocols specify standardized instructions, bounded target vocabularies, randomized prompt presentations, controlled baseline rest intervals, and millisecond-accurate event-marker synchronization. Explicitly separates imagined speech from overt phonation, whispered rehearsal, inner monologues, and general motor imagery to prevent paradigm conflation.

Focus: Experimental controls • Trial annotation • Task reproducibility
02 PREPROCESSING INTEGRITY

Artifact Characterization & Signal Quality

Quantifies contamination from ocular blinks, glossokinetic/facial myogenic activity, electrode contact impedance drifts, and ambient 50/60 Hz interference. Evaluates causal bandpass filtering, Independent Component Analysis (ICA), and surface Laplacian spatial transforms strictly against task-relevant neural epochs, verifying that artifact-rejection criteria do not attenuate genuine electrophysiological dynamics.

Focus: Signal-to-noise characterization • Artifact assessment • Data integrity
03 REPRESENTATION LEARNING

Spatiotemporal Representation Learning

Investigates convolutional feature extractors, temporal self-attention, and Conformer backbones as candidate architectures for modeling spectral dynamics and cross-electrode functional dependencies. Prioritizes rigorous baseline comparisons against classical linear decoders (CSP, Riemannian geometry), systematic component ablations, class-wise error matrices, and cross-session generalization.

Focus: Representation learning • Baseline comparison • Generalization
04 VALIDATION RIGOR

Leakage-Resistant Validation

Enforces strict partition boundaries isolating training, validation, and test datasets at the participant or longitudinal recording session level. Eliminates temporal autocorrelation leakage across overlapping sliding windows and repeated trials. Recommends reporting balanced accuracy, macro-F1, confusion matrices, and confidence intervals against empirical chance-level baselines.

Focus: Independent test sets • Statistical uncertainty • Cross-session evaluation
EXPERIMENTAL CONTROLS / REPRODUCIBILITY

From Experimental Design to Defensible Evidence

Reliable EEG-based neural decoding requires more than model performance. Experimental validity depends on controlled task design, signal-quality assessment, appropriate statistical analysis, and transparent reporting of methodological limitations. BrainWave’s research framework is intended to make these factors explicit throughout acquisition, preprocessing, model development, and evaluation.

A. ACQUISITION & RECORDING STANDARDIZATION Document electrode positions, reference and ground configuration, sampling frequency, analog filtering, acquisition hardware, event timing, recording duration, and channel-quality criteria. Report measured hardware characteristics only when supported by appropriate documentation or calibration.
B. PARTICIPANT VARIABILITY & EXPERIMENTAL CONFOUNDS Document relevant participant characteristics, task familiarity, session conditions, fatigue, movement, and other factors that may affect EEG recordings. Establish inclusion and exclusion criteria before analysis and report exclusions transparently.
C. STATISTICAL EVALUATION & BASELINES Compare candidate models against appropriate baselines, including relevant conventional EEG classification methods. Report the evaluation protocol, sample size, uncertainty estimates, class imbalance, and statistical limitations. Avoid selecting evaluation procedures solely because they maximize reported performance.
D. REPRODUCIBILITY, ETHICS & DATA GOVERNANCE Maintain versioned experimental protocols, preprocessing configurations, model parameters, software dependencies, and evaluation scripts. Require appropriate ethics approval and informed consent for human-participant research. Apply de-identification and controlled access to sensitive neural data, and share only materials authorized for release.

Methodological principle: Performance claims must be traceable to a defined experimental protocol, an independent evaluation procedure, and documented evidence. Proposed methods, implemented capabilities, and experimentally validated findings must be clearly distinguished.

01 // CORE RESEARCH STREAM

Neural Signal Translation & Speech Vectors

Investigating the relationship between multichannel electrophysiological dynamics, speech imagery, and structured digital outputs.

Decoding internally generated articulatory representations without physical vocal cord actuation forms the core investigational pathway of the BrainWave research initiative. By projecting multichannel scalp potentials into calibrated phonetic probability distributions, we analyze how distinct phonemic categories register across cortical topography.

Our experimental paradigms pair covert phonemic rehearsals with precise visual triggers, allowing acoustic-phonetic feature representations to be trained against time-synchronized EEG windows. The objective is to identify robust spectral and spatial markers that remain consistent across trials while suppressing physiological artifacts.

Rather than assuming unrestrained lexical reconstruction, current modeling isolates discrete phonetic boundary transitions, evaluating cross-entropy classification baselines against structured phoneme matrices.

  • Superior Temporal Gyrus (STG)↗
  • Premotor Cortex (PMC)↗
  • Broca's Area (IFG)↗
  • Primary Motor Strip (M1)↗

Speech imagery investigations focus on the silent internal generation of words and phrases without overt muscle activation. Isolating these sub-microvolt potentials requires identifying the electrophysiological signatures of linguistic intent.

Controlled trials evaluate event-related synchronization and desynchronization (ERS/ERD) patterns across sensorimotor electrodes, separating cognitive speech preparation from peripheral artifact interference.

INVESTIGATIVE REGIONS
  • Inferior Frontal Gyrus↗
  • Supplementary Motor Area (SMA)↗
  • Left Rolandic Cortex↗

Decoding motor intentions examines the neural planning stages preceding articulatory or assistive physical actions. Models evaluate preparatory slow cortical potentials and mu/beta rhythm fluctuations.

These investigations establish baseline decoding benchmarks for assistive human–computer control architectures, emphasizing low-latency command activation and minimal false-positive rates.

INVESTIGATIVE REGIONS

  • Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex↗

High-density spatial mapping investigates the topological projection of scalp electrode montages to underlying cortical coordinate systems using 10–20 standard positioning.

Surface Laplacian transformations and forward modeling techniques are explored to improve spatial resolution and isolate focal sources of speech-related electrophysiological rhythms.

Sub-millisecond data synchronization aligns multichannel EEG continuous streams with experimental trigger markers, audio cues, and stimulus display refresh events.

Utilizing protocol timestamps and jitter-corrected network streams, this layer ensures that spatiotemporal representations are trained exclusively on precisely phased neural epochs.
