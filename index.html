What is BrainWave?

BrainWave is Higgsion’s research initiative into non-invasive EEG-based neural interfaces, with a focus on speech-related neural activity and assistive communication. The system brings together electrophysiological acquisition, signal-quality assessment, spatiotemporal representation learning, and probabilistic decoding to investigate how neural activity associated with imagined speech can be translated into predefined, machine-interpretable outputs.

The research addresses fundamental challenges in EEG-based neural decoding, including low signal-to-noise ratio, physiological artifacts, inter-subject variability, limited training data, and generalization across recording sessions.

BrainWave investigates scalp-recorded electrical potentials using an eight-channel EEG research configuration. The workflow supports synchronized recording, electrode-contact assessment, reference management, and signal-quality monitoring during controlled speech-imagery experiments.

The pipeline investigates standardized montages, 24-bit analog front-end (AFE) amplification, and sub-millisecond event-marker synchronization while distinguishing nominal ADC resolution from effective resolution and monitoring ocular, muscular, and movement contamination.

Preprocessing and analytical workflows characterize task-related neural activity while accounting for physiological interference and experimental variability. Methods include continuous channel quality inspection, transparent rejection criteria, temporal filtering, and event-aligned segmentation.

Spatial decomposition and artifact characterization techniques are evaluated directly against task-relevant neural epochs to ensure filters do not distort underlying electrophysiological dynamics. Trials are organized under BIDS-compatible structures.

Evaluating whether machine-learning models can identify predefined speech-imagery targets from multichannel EEG recordings. Candidate architectures include convolutional feature extractors, attention-based temporal modeling, and Conformer backbones.

Research focuses on probabilistic confidence calibration, cross-session generalization, and rigorous benchmarking against classical baselines (CSP, Riemannian classifiers) using strict participant-isolated evaluation partitions.

BrainWave currently focuses on investigating EEG correlates of speech imagery and the feasibility of decoding predefined target classes. Potential future applications include alternative communication, assistive human–computer interaction, and neural feedback for motor rehabilitation.

These applications require task-specific experimental evidence and formal clinical validation before translational deployment.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE / CORE HARDWARE & DECODING VECTORS

The Engineering Stack for Neural Signal Interpretation

BrainWave investigates an integrated neural-decoding architecture spanning electrophysiological acquisition, signal conditioning, spatiotemporal representation learning, and probabilistic inference. Each layer addresses a distinct challenge in extracting task-relevant information from noisy, low-amplitude EEG signals.

01 INSTRUMENTATION

Electrophysiological Acquisition

Low-noise EEG acquisition with electrode-contact monitoring, synchronized sampling, reference management, and signal-integrity checks. Hardware performance is characterized through measured input-referred noise, effective resolution, usable bandwidth, and channel-level quality metrics.

Channel configuration • Sampling rate • Measured input noise
02 SIGNAL PROCESSING

Signal Conditioning and Artifact Modeling

Temporal filtering, channel-quality assessment, and characterization of ocular, muscular, motion, and environmental interference. Preprocessing methods are evaluated for their ability to suppress artifacts while preserving neural information relevant to the experimental task.

Filtering • Artifact assessment • Signal quality
03 NEURAL COMPUTATION

Spatiotemporal Representation Learning

Investigating convolutional encoders and Conformer-based architectures to learn temporal dependencies and cross-channel relationships in EEG associated with speech imagery. Learned representations are compared against established signal-processing and machine-learning baselines.

Temporal modeling • Cross-channel features • Representation learning
04 INFERENCE & PERSONALIZATION

Probabilistic Decoding and Adaptation

Mapping learned neural representations to predefined phonetic, lexical, or intent classes using probabilistic decoding. Subject-specific adaptation, confidence calibration, and cross-session generalization are evaluated using held-out data and controlled experimental protocols.

Probabilistic inference • Model adaptation • Generalization
PROCESSING PIPELINE / END-TO-END NEURAL INFERENCE

An End-to-End Pipeline for EEG-Based Neural Decoding

BrainWave investigates a structured decoding pipeline that transforms multichannel EEG recordings into machine-interpretable representations of speech-related neural activity. The workflow integrates signal acquisition, artifact assessment, spatiotemporal feature learning, and probabilistic inference to evaluate the feasibility of non-invasive imagined-speech decoding.

STEP 01

EEG Acquisition and Synchronization

Record multichannel scalp potentials during controlled speech-imagery tasks, with synchronized event markers, electrode metadata, and signal-quality monitoring. Preserve acquisition timestamps and experimental labels for reproducible downstream analysis.

STEP 02

Preprocessing and Artifact Assessment

Apply validated temporal filtering, channel-quality inspection, artifact characterization, and appropriate signal segmentation. Evaluate ocular, muscular, and motion-related interference while assessing whether preprocessing introduces information loss or task-dependent bias.

STEP 03

Spatiotemporal Representation Learning

Encode temporal dynamics and cross-channel relationships using convolutional and attention-based architectures, including Conformer-based models under investigation. Compare learned representations against established signal-processing and machine-learning baselines using appropriately separated training, validation, and test data.

STEP 04

Probabilistic Decoding and Output

Estimate probabilities over a predefined vocabulary, phonetic inventory, or intent-class set. Evaluate decoding errors, confidence calibration, generalization across sessions, and the contribution of any language-model prior. Where appropriate, incorporate uncertainty thresholds to reject unreliable predictions before generating an output.

CLINICAL INTEGRATION / TRANSLATIONAL APPLICATIONS

Translational Pathways in Neural Communication and Assistive Control

BrainWave investigates how non-invasive EEG-based neural decoding may support communication, neurorehabilitation research, and assistive human–computer interaction. These translational pathways focus on converting experimentally characterized neural activity into interpretable outputs while prioritizing reliability, user safety, and clinically meaningful evaluation.

01 ALTERNATIVE COMMUNICATION

Speech-Related Neural Decoding

Investigate whether EEG patterns associated with predefined imagined words or phonetic categories can support alternative communication interfaces. The research focuses on decoding feasibility, vocabulary constraints, inter-session consistency, and potential applications for individuals with severe speech impairments.

EVALUATION METRICS Vocabulary size • Decoding error rate
RESEARCH CRITERIA Information-transfer rate • Cross-session generalization
02 NEUROREHABILITATION RESEARCH

Neural Feedback and Motor Rehabilitation

Explore whether task-related neural decoding can provide feedback signals for future rehabilitation protocols, including motor imagery and neurorehabilitation paradigms. Investigate the reliability of neural feedback, task engagement, and the feasibility of integrating EEG-based interfaces into controlled rehabilitation studies.

EVALUATION METRICS Neural-feedback reliability • Task engagement
RESEARCH CRITERIA Protocol feasibility • Validated clinical outcome measures
03 ASSISTIVE HUMAN–COMPUTER INTERACTION

Neural Intent-to-Command Interfaces

Investigate mapping decoded, predefined intent classes to computer interaction and assistive-device commands. The research emphasizes confidence-aware decision-making, user-controlled confirmation, low-latency communication, and safeguards against unintended command activation.

EVALUATION METRICS Command accuracy • False activation rate
RESEARCH CRITERIA End-to-end response latency • Operational safety
RESEARCH INTEGRITY / BENCHMARKS & REPRODUCIBILITY

Reproducible Experiments. Transparent Evaluation. Independent Validation.

Scientific progress in brain–computer interfaces depends on reproducible experiments, rigorous evaluation, and transparent reporting. BrainWave aims to establish a research framework for assessing EEG-based neural decoding through documented experimental protocols, leakage-resistant evaluation, comparative benchmarks, and responsible data governance.

Protocol and Dataset Documentation

Document participant eligibility, sample size, experimental tasks, electrode montage, acquisition parameters, event timing, exclusion criteria, and preprocessing procedures. Adopt BIDS-compatible EEG data organization where applicable to improve interoperability and facilitate independent analysis.

Experimental protocols • Dataset metadata • BIDS compatibility

Leakage-Resistant Evaluation

Establish clearly separated training, validation, and test datasets using participant-level or session-level partitions appropriate to the research question. Compare proposed models against established baselines and report class-wise performance, uncertainty estimates, confidence intervals, and relevant statistical limitations.

Independent test sets • Baseline comparisons • Statistical uncertainty

Reproducible Models and Pipelines

Maintain versioned datasets, model configurations, preprocessing parameters, software dependencies, random seeds, and evaluation scripts. Document architectural choices and experimental conditions so that independent researchers can reproduce reported results and distinguish computational reproducibility from external replication.

Version control • Model configurations • Reproducible evaluation

Privacy and Research Governance

Establish appropriate informed consent, ethics approval, de-identification procedures, access controls, and explicit data-sharing conditions. Protect sensitive neural recordings and release datasets, model artifacts, and research materials only when legally and ethically authorized.

Research ethics • Neural-data privacy • Controlled data access
