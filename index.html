BrainWave is Higgsion’s research initiative into non-invasive neural interfaces for decoding speech-related neural activity and translating it into structured digital outputs. By combining electroencephalography (EEG), signal processing, spatiotemporal representation learning, and probabilistic decoding, BrainWave investigates whether internally generated linguistic intent can be mapped to machine-interpretable representations without surgical implantation.

The research focuses on the neural correlates of imagined speech, robust feature extraction from low-amplitude electrophysiological signals, and adaptive decoding across trials, sessions, and individuals. The long-term objective is to develop an experimentally validated communication interface for assistive technologies and human–computer interaction, with translational applications subject to clinical and technical validation.

01

Neural Signal Characterization

Investigating the temporal and spatial structure of EEG activity associated with speech imagery, including physiological artifacts and inter-subject variability.

02

Adaptive Neural Decoding

Developing machine-learning representations that distinguish task-related neural activity from noise while addressing subject-specific and session-dependent signal variation.

03

Assistive Communication Research

Exploring neural interfaces for alternative communication, digital interaction, and future assistive applications for people with speech or motor impairments.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE / CORE HARDWARE & DECODING VECTORS

The Engineering Stack for Neural Signal Interpretation

BrainWave investigates an integrated neural-decoding architecture spanning electrophysiological acquisition, signal conditioning, spatiotemporal representation learning, and probabilistic inference. Each layer addresses a distinct challenge in extracting task-relevant information from noisy, low-amplitude EEG signals.

01 INSTRUMENTATION

Electrophysiological Acquisition

Low-noise EEG acquisition with electrode-contact monitoring, synchronized sampling, reference management, and signal-integrity checks. Hardware performance is characterized through measured input-referred noise, effective resolution, usable bandwidth, and channel-level quality metrics.

Channel configuration • Sampling rate • Measured input noise
02 SIGNAL PROCESSING

Signal Conditioning and Artifact Modeling

Temporal filtering, channel-quality assessment, and characterization of ocular, muscular, motion, and environmental interference. Preprocessing methods are evaluated for their ability to suppress artifacts while preserving neural information relevant to the experimental task.

Filtering • Artifact assessment • Signal quality
03 NEURAL COMPUTATION

Spatiotemporal Representation Learning

Investigating convolutional encoders and Conformer-based architectures to learn temporal dependencies and cross-channel relationships in EEG associated with speech imagery. Learned representations are compared against established signal-processing and machine-learning baselines.

Temporal modeling • Cross-channel features • Representation learning
04 INFERENCE & PERSONALIZATION

Probabilistic Decoding and Adaptation

Mapping learned neural representations to predefined phonetic, lexical, or intent classes using probabilistic decoding. Subject-specific adaptation, confidence calibration, and cross-session generalization are evaluated using held-out data and controlled experimental protocols.

Probabilistic inference • Model adaptation • Generalization
PROCESSING PIPELINE / END-TO-END TELEMETRY DECODING

From Cortical Intent to Output

How covert articulatory intentions move through filtering, spatial separation, and language inference.

STEP 01

Acquisition

8-Channel differential EEG montage capture at 1 kHz with continuous impedance logging.

STEP 02

Spatial Artifact Rejection

Surface Laplacian & FastICA isolate ocular blinks and myogenic noise without attenuating speech signals.

STEP 03

Conformer Encoding

Sliding 400ms context windows extract temporal trajectories and spatial cross-channel co-activations.

STEP 04

Intent Synthesis

WFST beam search filters phonetic probabilities into stable assistive text or device actuation commands.
